## My Starting Point (Before the Internship)

Before this internship, I had been freelancing for several years. It was my main source of income while I was still a student. I got good at managing projects solo, talking directly to clients, and pushing through tight deadlines.

Because of that background, I entered this internship with a very independent mindset. I expected clear instructions, strict deliverables, and to mostly work on my own. I was ready to grind technically — but I wasn’t prepared for how much I would grow through collaboration.

---

## What Surprised Me Most

### 1. The Freedom We Were Given
One of the biggest surprises was how much freedom we had in the exercises. Instead of being told exactly what to build, we were encouraged to choose our own projects using React and Django. 

This freedom made learning way more fun and meaningful. I felt real ownership over what I was building, which kept me motivated throughout the program.

### 2. Having a Co-intern Who’s Also a Friend
I was lucky that my co-intern was already a good friend of mine. Because we already knew each other, communication was natural and effortless from day one.

We could easily bounce ideas, debug together, and support each other during tough moments. Having that built-in relationship removed a lot of the usual awkwardness of starting somewhere new.

### 3. The Mentoring & Collaboration Culture
This was my first real experience with proper mentoring and team collaboration in years. After working solo as a freelancer, I had almost forgotten how valuable it is to learn from others in real time.

The mentors were patient, approachable, and genuinely invested in our growth. I looked forward to our discussions and code reviews more than I expected.

---

## The Turning Point: My Setback Moment

Midway through the internship, I hit a wall. I had chosen a technology stack that seemed promising but ended up being the wrong choice for what I needed. I had to throw away a big portion of my work and rewrite it from scratch.

I was stressed. Coming from freelance, I expected harsh feedback or even some form of punishment for missing the deadline. 

Instead, the team surprised me in the best way possible:
- They gave me a reasonable extension.
- Mentors regularly checked in and asked, “What do you need?” and “How can we support you?”
- The feedback was constructive, never blame-oriented.

This experience was eye-opening. It showed me that in a healthy team environment, mistakes are treated as learning opportunities — not career-ending failures. The psychological safety I felt here was something I had rarely experienced before.

## What I Enjoyed Most (Highlights)

As the internship nears its end, I can confidently say I've grown to genuinely love collaboration.

Working closely with my co-intern felt rewarding — we celebrated small wins together and helped each other through challenges. The senior developers and mentors created such a supportive atmosphere that I never felt scared to ask questions or show my work. Even our new PM brought a fresh perspective on how processes and project management should work.

After years of solo freelance work, I discovered how much more fulfilling it is to build something together with others. The combination of technical learning and human connection made this internship one of the best experiences I've had.

## Key Lessons & Advice for Future Interns

Here are the most important things I wish someone had told me before starting:

### Technical Lessons
- Take advantage of the freedom — don’t be afraid to experiment with different tools and approaches.
- Validate your tech choices early. It’s better to pivot sooner than later.
- Don’t over-engineer. Focus on learning and delivering value first.

### Collaboration & Soft Skills
- Build relationships early. Talk to your co-intern, mentors, and team members.
- Ask for help without hesitation — people here actually want to support you.
- Observe how seniors communicate, especially during code reviews and meetings.

### Mindset Shifts
- Progress > Perfection. It’s okay to make mistakes.
- Collaboration makes you grow faster than working alone.
- A supportive team can turn setbacks into valuable learning moments.

### Practical Tips
- Prepare your development environment before Day 1.
- Take notes during mentoring sessions — you’ll learn a lot.
- Document your progress. It helps when writing your own blueprint later!
